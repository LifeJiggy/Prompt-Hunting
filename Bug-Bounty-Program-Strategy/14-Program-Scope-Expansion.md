# Strategy Guide: Program Scope Expansion

## Expert Role

You are a strategic scope planning specialist focused on expanding bug bounty program coverage to maximize security value while managing organizational risk and researcher engagement efficiency. Your expertise encompasses the complex decision-making process of determining which assets, vulnerability classes, and testing methodologies should be included in program scope to optimize the balance between security benefit discovery and operational management burden.

Your analytical framework combines threat modeling, risk assessment, and market analysis to develop scope expansion strategies that systematically increase program value. You understand that scope decisions significantly influence researcher engagement patterns, vulnerability discovery potential, and program management complexity. Your methodology involves evaluating expansion opportunities through multiple lenses including security value creation, operational feasibility, researcher attraction potential, and competitive positioning impact.

You maintain comprehensive databases mapping organizational attack surfaces to vulnerability potential, researcher interest levels, and competitive program scope benchmarks. Your analysis identifies high-value expansion opportunities that attract specialized researcher communities while managing scope creep that could overwhelm program management capabilities. You specialize in phased expansion approaches that build organizational scope management capabilities while progressively increasing security coverage.

## Overview

Program scope expansion represents the strategic process of increasing the range of assets, systems, vulnerability classes, and testing methodologies covered by a bug bounty program. This discipline requires careful balancing of security value creation against operational complexity, researcher management burden, and organizational risk tolerance. Effective scope expansion systematically increases program security coverage while maintaining program management effectiveness and researcher engagement quality.

The scope expansion decision process must consider multiple interconnected factors including organizational asset criticality, vulnerability discovery potential, researcher expertise requirements, legal and regulatory constraints, and competitive positioning objectives. Expansion that is too aggressive may overwhelm program management capabilities and reduce overall program effectiveness, while overly conservative scope limits may leave significant security value undiscovered and reduce researcher interest in the program.

Successful scope expansion strategies typically follow phased approaches that build organizational capabilities incrementally while delivering measurable security value at each stage. The most effective practitioners develop systematic evaluation frameworks that prioritize expansion opportunities based on risk-adjusted security value, operational feasibility, and strategic alignment. This approach transforms scope expansion from ad-hoc decision-making into a strategic capability that consistently increases program security contribution.

## Strategic Framework

### Phase 1: Current Scope Assessment and Gap Analysis

The first phase establishes comprehensive visibility into current program scope, identifies coverage gaps, and evaluates expansion potential across different asset categories.

**Asset Inventory and Classification:**
- Create comprehensive inventory of organizational assets including applications, APIs, infrastructure, and mobile applications
- Classify assets by criticality, exposure level, and vulnerability discovery potential
- Map asset interdependencies and potential attack chain opportunities
- Identify assets currently excluded from program scope and reasons for exclusion

**Current Scope Effectiveness Analysis:**
- Measure vulnerability discovery rates across current scope categories
- Assess researcher engagement patterns and preferences within current scope
- Analyze vulnerability class distribution and identify underrepresented categories
- Evaluate program management capacity and efficiency within current scope constraints

**Gap Identification Methodology:**
- Compare current scope against comprehensive organizational attack surface
- Identify high-value assets excluded from scope due to historical rather than strategic reasons
- Assess vulnerability classes not adequately covered by current scope definition
- Map potential attack paths that span current scope boundaries

**Expansion Opportunity Prioritization:**
- Rank expansion opportunities by risk-adjusted security value creation potential
- Assess operational feasibility and resource requirements for each expansion option
- Evaluate researcher attraction potential and competitive positioning impact
- Consider organizational readiness and change management requirements

### Phase 2: Expansion Strategy Development

The second phase develops comprehensive expansion strategies that balance security value creation against operational and organizational constraints.

**Phased Expansion Roadmap:**
- Design multi-stage expansion plan with clear milestones and success criteria
- Sequence expansion initiatives to build organizational capabilities incrementally
- Align expansion timeline with organizational readiness and resource availability
- Create decision gates that enable scope expansion based on demonstrated success

**Risk Management Framework:**
- Develop risk assessment methodology for scope expansion decisions
- Create mitigation strategies for potential risks associated with expanded scope
- Establish monitoring mechanisms that detect expansion-related issues early
- Design rollback procedures for scope expansions that create unexpected problems

**Operational Readiness Planning:**
- Assess current program management capacity and identify expansion constraints
- Develop training and knowledge building for expanded scope management
- Create process documentation and tooling for new scope categories
- Establish quality assurance mechanisms for expanded scope operations

**Researcher Engagement Strategy:**
- Design scope expansion communications that maximize researcher awareness and engagement
- Create incentive structures that encourage researcher exploration of new scope areas
- Develop expertise requirements and qualification criteria for specialized scope
- Establish collaboration mechanisms that support researcher success in expanded scope

### Phase 3: Implementation and Validation

The third phase executes scope expansion decisions while validating security value creation and operational effectiveness.

**Controlled Expansion Execution:**
- Implement scope expansions through controlled rollout with monitoring and measurement
- Create feedback mechanisms that capture researcher response and operational impact
- Establish success criteria and measurement approaches for each expansion initiative
- Develop communication plans that keep stakeholders informed of expansion progress

**Security Value Validation:**
- Measure vulnerability discovery impact of scope expansions
- Assess researcher engagement changes resulting from expanded scope
- Analyze vulnerability severity and type distribution across expanded scope
- Quantify risk reduction and security improvement attributable to scope expansion

**Operational Impact Assessment:**
- Monitor program management workload and efficiency changes
- Assess triage and remediation process performance under expanded scope
- Evaluate researcher quality and submission validity across new scope areas

### Phase 4: Optimization and Continuous Expansion

The final phase optimizes expanded scope operations and establishes frameworks for ongoing scope evolution.

**Scope Performance Optimization:**
- Analyze expanded scope performance data to identify optimization opportunities
- Refine scope boundaries based on observed researcher behavior and vulnerability patterns
- Adjust scope definitions to improve researcher engagement and vulnerability discovery
- Optimize scope management processes based on operational experience

**Continuous Expansion Framework:**
- Establish ongoing scope review processes that identify new expansion opportunities
- Create trigger mechanisms that initiate scope expansion evaluation when conditions change
- Develop capability building that enables progressive scope expansion over time
- Build institutional knowledge that improves scope expansion decision-making

**Strategic Alignment Maintenance:**
- Regularly reassess scope strategy alignment with organizational security objectives
- Adjust scope priorities based on evolving threat landscape and business requirements
- Maintain scope expansion momentum while avoiding overextension and quality degradation
- Create governance processes that ensure scope decisions support broader strategic goals

## Real-World Examples

### Example 1: API Security Scope Expansion

A SaaS company expanded its bug bounty program scope to include comprehensive API security testing after discovering that 73% of their critical vulnerabilities were discovered through API endpoints that were not adequately covered in the original program scope. The expansion was driven by analysis showing that traditional web application testing was missing the majority of high-severity vulnerabilities in their modern, API-driven architecture.

**Scope Expansion Analysis:**
Initial program scope focused on web application interfaces and excluded direct API testing due to concerns about potential service disruption and data exposure. However, analysis of internal security testing results and incident reports revealed that API endpoints were the primary attack vector for sophisticated adversaries. The security team estimated that excluding API testing left approximately 60% of the attack surface uncovered.

**Phased Implementation Strategy:**
The expansion was implemented in three phases. Phase 1 introduced read-only API testing for authenticated researchers with established reputation. Phase 2 expanded to include write operations for researchers with specific API security expertise. Phase 3 opened comprehensive API testing including business logic testing for the full researcher community with appropriate safeguards.

**Technical Safeguards:**
The company implemented several technical safeguards to manage API testing risks including rate limiting for bounty hunters, data isolation mechanisms for test accounts, automated detection of excessive API usage, and real-time monitoring for potential service impact. These safeguards enabled comprehensive API testing while maintaining production system stability.

**Security Value Realization:**
Within the first six months of full API scope expansion, researchers discovered 47 critical API vulnerabilities including authentication bypass, authorization flaws, and data exposure issues that internal testing had missed. The total bounty expenditure of $285,000 was estimated to have prevented potential losses exceeding $12 million based on the severity and potential impact of discovered vulnerabilities.

### Example 2: Cloud Infrastructure Scope Extension

A financial technology company expanded program scope to include cloud infrastructure security testing after recognizing that cloud misconfigurations represented their highest-risk vulnerability category. The expansion required careful planning due to regulatory requirements and the complexity of cloud security testing.

**Scope Definition Challenges:**
Defining appropriate scope for cloud infrastructure testing required navigating complex boundaries between application infrastructure, shared responsibility models, and regulatory compliance requirements. The company needed to exclude certain infrastructure components while including others based on their security criticality and testing feasibility.

**Researcher Qualification Requirements:**
The expanded scope required researchers with specialized cloud security expertise including AWS, Azure, and GCP platform knowledge. The company implemented qualification requirements including demonstrated cloud security experience, platform-specific certifications, and successful completion of scope-specific training modules.

**Compliance Integration:**
The scope expansion was designed to comply with financial services regulations including SOC 2, PCI DSS, and regulatory guidance on cloud security testing. This required legal review of researcher agreements, data handling procedures, and testing boundaries to ensure compliance while enabling effective security testing.

**Measurable Outcomes:**
The cloud infrastructure scope expansion resulted in discovery of 23 critical misconfigurations including publicly exposed databases, overly permissive IAM policies, and insecure network configurations. The total investment of $150,000 in bounties and program management was estimated to have reduced cloud security risk by $8.5 million based on potential exposure and regulatory penalty avoidance.

### Example 3: Mobile Application Scope Addition

An e-commerce platform added comprehensive mobile application security testing to its bug bounty program after internal analysis revealed significant gaps in mobile security coverage. The expansion addressed both iOS and Android platforms including backend API security specific to mobile implementations.

**Mobile-Specific Scope Considerations:**
Mobile application scope required definition of testing boundaries including application binary analysis, runtime manipulation, device-specific testing, and backend API interactions. The company developed detailed scope documentation that specified allowed testing techniques, data handling requirements, and platform-specific restrictions.

**Platform Divergence Management:**
The scope expansion needed to address differences between iOS and Android security testing including platform-specific vulnerability classes, testing tool requirements, and researcher expertise needs. The company created separate scope definitions and qualification requirements for each platform while maintaining consistent overall program standards.

**Researcher Enablement:**
Successful mobile scope expansion required significant researcher enablement including provision of test applications, device access programs, documentation for mobile-specific functionality, and dedicated support channels for mobile security researchers. These investments reduced barriers to entry and accelerated mobile vulnerability discovery.

**Security Improvement Results:**
Mobile scope expansion uncovered 38 critical vulnerabilities across both platforms including insecure data storage, broken authentication, insufficient transport encryption, and platform-specific implementation flaws. The mobile-specific bounty budget of $200,000 addressed security risks estimated at $6.2 million in potential losses from mobile application compromises.

### Example 4: Third-Party Integration Scope Expansion

A healthcare technology company expanded scope to include security testing of third-party integrations and APIs after discovering that several critical vulnerabilities originated in integration points rather than core application code. The expansion addressed the growing attack surface created by numerous third-party service integrations.

**Third-Party Scope Complexity:**
Defining appropriate scope for third-party integration testing required balancing security testing needs against legal agreements with third-party vendors, potential impact on third-party systems, and researcher access limitations. The company developed tiered scope that differentiated between fully authorized testing and restricted testing requiring additional approval.

**Legal and Contractual Framework:**
The scope expansion required legal review and modification of researcher agreements, third-party vendor contracts, and data processing agreements. The company worked with legal counsel to develop testing frameworks that enabled security research while respecting contractual obligations and liability boundaries.

**Researcher Guidance and Support:**
Third-party integration testing required specialized guidance for researchers including documentation of integration architectures, authentication mechanisms, and testing boundaries. The company created detailed testing guides and established communication channels for researcher questions about scope boundaries and testing approaches.

**Ecosystem Security Improvement:**
The expanded scope discovered 19 critical vulnerabilities in third-party integrations including authentication bypass, data exposure, and injection vulnerabilities. These findings improved not only the company's security posture but also contributed to third-party vendor security improvements, creating broader ecosystem security value.

### Example 5: IoT and Hardware Scope Addition

A consumer electronics company expanded its bug bounty program to include IoT device and hardware security testing, recognizing that connected devices represented their fastest-growing attack surface. The expansion required development of entirely new testing methodologies and researcher engagement approaches.

**Physical Testing Requirements:**
IoT and hardware testing required physical device access, specialized testing equipment, and hardware reverse engineering capabilities. The company developed a device lending program that provided researchers with test devices while establishing clear guidelines for device handling, testing boundaries, and return procedures.

**Testing Methodology Development:**
The scope expansion required creation of comprehensive testing methodology documentation covering firmware analysis, hardware interface testing, wireless protocol security, and cloud service integration testing. These methodologies were developed in collaboration with IoT security researchers and adapted based on early testing feedback.

**Researcher Community Development:**
IoT security research required specialized expertise that limited the initial researcher pool. The company invested in community development through dedicated communication channels, technical workshops, and collaboration with IoT security research communities to build the researcher base needed for effective coverage.

**Product Security Enhancement:**
IoT scope expansion discovered 31 critical vulnerabilities across firmware, hardware, and cloud integration components including insecure update mechanisms, hardcoded credentials, and insufficient encryption implementation. These findings enabled product security improvements that protected millions of deployed devices and enhanced customer trust in the company's security commitment.

## Best Practices

### Practice 1: Risk-Based Scope Prioritization

Effective scope expansion requires systematic prioritization based on risk reduction potential and security value creation rather than ad-hoc decisions driven by convenience or historical precedent.

**Threat Modeling Integration:**
Incorporate comprehensive threat modeling into scope expansion decisions that identify highest-risk assets and vulnerability classes. Map organizational attack surfaces to potential threat scenarios and prioritize scope expansion that addresses highest-impact risks. Use threat intelligence to anticipate emerging attack patterns that should inform scope expansion priorities.

**Risk Quantification Framework:**
Develop quantitative risk assessment models that estimate security value creation for different scope expansion options. Consider factors including asset criticality, vulnerability likelihood, potential impact, and existing security controls when calculating risk reduction value. Create decision frameworks that prioritize scope expansions offering highest risk-adjusted returns.

**Business Impact Alignment:**
Align scope expansion priorities with business objectives and risk tolerance. Consider regulatory requirements, customer expectations, and competitive positioning when evaluating expansion opportunities. Ensure scope decisions support broader organizational goals beyond pure security metrics.

### Practice 2: Phased Expansion Implementation

Gradual scope expansion enables organizational capability building while managing risk and maintaining program quality across expansion initiatives.

**Incremental Capability Building:**
Design expansion phases that build organizational capabilities for scope management progressively. Start with lower-complexity expansions that establish processes and demonstrate value before advancing to more challenging scope additions. Create learning opportunities that inform subsequent expansion phases.

**Success Validation Gates:**
Establish clear success criteria and validation gates between expansion phases. Require demonstrated success in current scope additions before progressing to more complex expansions. Create decision frameworks that enable scope expansion acceleration or deceleration based on observed outcomes.

**Resource Allocation Alignment:**
Match expansion phasing with organizational resource availability and capability development. Avoid expansion initiatives that exceed current operational capacity regardless of potential security value. Create resource development plans that enable scope expansion at sustainable rates.

### Practice 3: Comprehensive Scope Documentation

Clear, detailed scope documentation reduces researcher confusion, manages expectations, and enables consistent program management across expanded coverage areas.

**Scope Definition Standards:**
Create standardized documentation formats for scope definitions that ensure consistency and completeness across different asset categories and vulnerability classes. Include explicit inclusions, exclusions, testing methodology guidelines, and data handling requirements for each scope category. Maintain documentation currency through regular review and update processes.

**Researcher Communication Optimization:**
Design scope documentation for researcher usability with clear language, practical examples, and easy navigation. Create multiple documentation formats including detailed technical specifications, quick reference guides, and visual scope maps. Establish feedback mechanisms that identify documentation improvement opportunities.

**Management Process Documentation:**
Document program management processes for expanded scope including triage procedures, escalation paths, and quality assurance mechanisms. Create operational runbooks that enable consistent scope management across different program team members. Establish knowledge management systems that preserve institutional scope management expertise.

### Practice 4: Researcher Enablement and Support

Successful scope expansion requires proactive researcher enablement that reduces barriers to entry and supports researcher success in new scope areas.

**Training and Education Development:**
Create training materials and educational resources for expanded scope areas that help researchers develop necessary expertise. Establish certification or qualification programs that validate researcher capabilities for specialized scope. Partner with researcher communities to develop and validate training content.

**Tool and Resource Provisioning:**
Provide researchers with tools, test environments, and resources needed for effective testing in expanded scope areas. Create sandbox environments that enable safe exploration of expanded scope without production system risks. Establish resource sharing mechanisms that reduce researcher investment barriers for specialized testing.

**Technical Support Infrastructure:**
Develop dedicated support channels for expanded scope testing that provide researcher assistance with technical questions, scope interpretation, and testing methodology guidance. Create responsive support processes that enable researcher progress without excessive delays. Establish expert consultation mechanisms for complex scope-specific challenges.

### Practice 5: Quality Assurance and Control

Expanded scope requires robust quality assurance mechanisms that maintain program effectiveness and researcher experience standards as scope complexity increases.

**Submission Quality Monitoring:**
Implement enhanced quality monitoring for expanded scope submissions that detects issues early and enables rapid corrective action. Create quality metrics specific to different scope categories that enable targeted improvement. Establish quality feedback mechanisms that help researchers improve submission quality in new scope areas.

**Triage Process Optimization:**

**Program Management Oversight:**
Increase program management oversight during scope expansion implementation to detect and address issues quickly. Create monitoring dashboards that provide visibility into expanded scope performance. ### Practice 6: Legal and Compliance Framework Adaptation

**Legal Agreement Updates:**

Scope expansion effectiveness must be measured and optimized through systematic performance tracking and continuous improvement processes.

**Expansion Impact Measurement:**

Track metrics including vulnerability discovery rates, severity distributions, and researcher engagement across expanded scope areas. Compare actual outcomes against expansion projections to validate decision-making effectiveness.

## Common Mistakes

### Mistake 1: Scope Expansion Without Operational Readiness

Expanding scope before developing adequate operational capabilities creates quality degradation, researcher frustration, and potential security risks from inadequate program management.

**Problematic Behavior:** Organizations rush to expand scope to match competitor offerings or respond to researcher requests without ensuring they have the triage capacity, technical expertise, and management processes needed for effective scope management. This leads to delayed response times, poor researcher experience, and missed vulnerability discoveries.

**Corrective Approach:** Conduct thorough operational readiness assessments before scope expansion. Develop required capabilities including triage expertise, management processes, and technical infrastructure before expanding scope. Implement phased expansion that builds capabilities incrementally while demonstrating value at each stage.

### Mistake 2: Overly Broad Scope Definition

Defining scope too broadly creates ambiguity, increases management complexity, and may include assets or activities that create unacceptable risks without proportional security benefits.

**Problematic Behavior:** Organizations include every possible asset and vulnerability class in scope without considering management feasibility, researcher expertise requirements, or risk implications. This creates scope definitions that are difficult to manage, confusing for researchers, and potentially risky.

**Corrective Approach:** Define scope with precision that enables clear researcher guidance and consistent management. Consider operational capacity and management complexity when defining scope boundaries. Create scope tiers that enable progressive expansion based on demonstrated management capability.

### Mistake 3: Inadequate Researcher Communication

Scope changes that are not clearly communicated to the researcher community result in missed engagement opportunities, scope confusion, and potential out-of-scope testing that creates legal and operational issues.

**Problematic Behavior:** Organizations modify scope without proactive researcher communication, assuming that updated documentation is sufficient. Researchers continue operating under old scope assumptions, missing new opportunities or inadvertently testing out-of-scope assets.

**Corrective Approach:** Develop comprehensive communication plans for scope changes that reach researchers through multiple channels. Create clear, prominent announcements for scope modifications with detailed guidance for researchers. Establish feedback mechanisms that confirm researcher understanding of scope changes.

### Mistake 4: Ignoring Legal and Compliance Implications

Scope expansion without appropriate legal and compliance review can create regulatory violations, contractual breaches, and organizational liability that outweigh security benefits.

**Problematic Behavior:** Organizations expand scope to include new asset categories without reviewing legal implications including data protection requirements, third-party agreements, and regulatory compliance obligations. This can result in unauthorized testing activities or data exposure that creates legal risks.

**Corrective Approach:** Establish legal and compliance review processes for all scope expansions. Create compliance checklists for different expansion types that ensure consistent review. Engage legal counsel for scope expansions involving regulatory-sensitive areas or novel testing activities.

### Mistake 5: Scope Creep Without Value Validation

Gradual, uncontrolled scope expansion that occurs without validation of security value creation can overwhelm program management capabilities without proportional security benefits.

**Problematic Behavior:** Organizations progressively expand scope through small, incremental additions without measuring whether each expansion delivers sufficient security value to justify management complexity. This scope creep gradually degrades program effectiveness as management capacity is spread across too many scope areas.

**Corrective Approach:** Implement formal scope expansion governance that requires value justification for each addition. Create scope change control processes that evaluate security benefit against management cost. Regularly review expanded scope to identify and remove coverage that isn't delivering sufficient value.

### Mistake 6: Neglecting Researcher Expertise Requirements

Scope expansion into technically specialized areas without considering researcher expertise requirements can result in inadequate testing quality and missed vulnerabilities.

**Problematic Behavior:** Organizations expand scope to include specialized technology areas like blockchain, IoT, or cloud infrastructure without establishing researcher qualification requirements or providing necessary training resources. This attracts researchers without appropriate expertise, resulting in low-quality testing and missed critical vulnerabilities.

**Corrective Approach:** Assess researcher expertise requirements for expanded scope areas and establish appropriate qualification mechanisms. Provide training resources and enablement for researchers to develop necessary expertise. Create scope tiers that differentiate between general and specialized testing requirements.

### Mistake 7: Failure to Adapt Management Processes

Expanding scope without adapting program management processes, tools, and team capabilities creates operational inefficiencies and quality problems that undermine expansion value.

**Problematic Behavior:** Organizations add new scope categories without updating triage procedures, escalation paths, training programs, and management tools to accommodate expanded requirements. Existing processes optimized for original scope become inefficient or ineffective for expanded coverage.

**Corrective Approach:** Adapt program management processes, tools, and team capabilities to accommodate scope expansion requirements. Create operational runbooks for new scope areas that enable consistent management. Invest in training and development that builds team capabilities for expanded scope management.

## Advanced Techniques

### Technique 1: Predictive Scope Value Modeling

This advanced technique develops predictive models that estimate security value creation for potential scope expansions before implementation.

**Value Prediction Methodology:**
Create statistical models that predict vulnerability discovery rates, severity distributions, and researcher engagement for different scope expansion options based on historical data and comparable scope characteristics. Incorporate factors including asset complexity, exposure level, technology stack, and competitive scope coverage into prediction models.

**Risk-Adjusted Value Calculation:**
Develop risk-adjusted value metrics that consider both expected security benefits and potential risks or costs associated with scope expansion. Create comprehensive cost-benefit analysis frameworks that include direct bounty costs, management overhead, and risk mitigation expenses against expected vulnerability discovery value.

**Decision Support Integration:**
Translate predictive models into decision support tools that enable systematic scope expansion prioritization. Create visualization capabilities that illustrate predicted outcomes for different expansion scenarios. Establish model validation processes that compare predictions against actual outcomes to improve accuracy.

### Technique 2: Dynamic Scope Adjustment

This technique creates adaptive scope mechanisms that automatically adjust coverage based on real-time conditions and performance indicators.

**Performance-Based Scope Optimization:**
Implement scope definitions that automatically adjust based on observed researcher behavior, vulnerability discovery patterns, and management capacity utilization. Create dynamic boundaries that expand in high-performing areas and contract in underperforming areas based on objective criteria.

**Market-Responsive Scope Adaptation:**
Develop scope adjustment mechanisms that respond to market conditions including researcher supply, competitive scope offerings, and emerging vulnerability trends. Create real-time scope optimization that maximizes researcher engagement and security value based on current market dynamics.

**Capacity-Aware Scope Management:**
Implement scope management systems that automatically adjust coverage based on current program management capacity and operational constraints. Create load-balancing mechanisms that optimize scope coverage within available management resources.

This technique optimizes scope decisions across multiple programs or business units to maximize overall organizational security value.

**Portfolio-Level Scope Planning:**
Develop scope expansion strategies that consider the complete portfolio of organizational programs to avoid duplication and identify synergies. Create cross-program coordination mechanisms that ensure consistent scope decisions across related assets and systems.

**Resource Sharing Optimization:**
Identify opportunities for shared scope management resources, processes, and expertise across multiple programs. Create centralized capabilities that serve multiple programs while maintaining appropriate scope differentiation.

**Comprehensive Coverage Planning:**
Develop organizational attack surface models that identify the most valuable scope expansions across all programs. Prioritize expansion initiatives that provide security value across multiple programs or address critical gaps in organizational coverage.

### Technique 4: Researcher-Driven Scope Evolution

This technique creates mechanisms that enable researcher input to inform and drive scope expansion decisions.

**Community Scope Proposals:**
Establish formal processes for researchers to propose scope expansions based on observed attack surfaces, vulnerability patterns, and security testing opportunities. Create evaluation frameworks that assess researcher proposals against organizational priorities and feasibility criteria.

**Collaborative Scope Design:**
Involve experienced researchers in scope expansion design to ensure researcher perspective is incorporated into scope decisions. Create advisory mechanisms that leverage researcher expertise for scope optimization while maintaining organizational control over scope decisions.

**Feedback-Driven Iteration:**
Implement continuous feedback collection from researchers about scope effectiveness, gaps, and improvement opportunities. Create iterative scope refinement processes that incorporate researcher insights while maintaining strategic alignment and operational feasibility.
