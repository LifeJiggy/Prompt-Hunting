## Overview

Bug bounty hunting is not just about finding vulnerabilities — it is about **finding the right vulnerabilities in the right programs at the right time**. The difference between a hunter earning $50K/year and one earning $500K/year is rarely skill alone. It is **strategy**.

This domain provides 50 comprehensive modules covering every aspect of bug bounty program strategy, from initial selection through long-term relationship management and advanced intelligence gathering. Each module is self-contained yet interconnected, forming a complete strategic framework.

### What You Will Learn

- How to evaluate and select programs that match your skills and maximize ROI
- Time management techniques specific to bug bounty hunting
- How to build and maintain relationships with program owners
- Advanced intelligence gathering on program trends and competitor behavior
- Risk assessment methodologies for individual programs
- Collaboration strategies that compound returns
- When to exit a program and how to do it strategically

### Who This Is For

- **New Hunters**: Start here to avoid common pitfalls and accelerate your learning curve
- **Intermediate Hunters**: Use these modules to optimize your current strategy and increase earnings
- **Advanced Hunters**: Leverage advanced modules for competitive intelligence and relationship building
- **Team Leads**: Apply framework-level thinking to allocate team resources across programs

## Why This Domain Exists

Most bug bounty resources focus on **technical exploitation** — how to find XSS, SSRF, IDOR, and other vulnerability classes. Very few address the **strategic layer** that determines which programs to target, how to allocate time, and how to build sustainable hunting practices.

This domain fills that gap. It is built from analysis of:
- 500+ disclosed bug bounty reports across major platforms
- Reward data from HackerOne, Bugcrowd, Intigriti, and Immunefi
- Program lifecycle patterns observed over 3+ years of active hunting
- Interviews and public statements from top-earning hunters
- Platform documentation and program policy analysis

The result is a systematic approach to the **business side** of bug bounty hunting — treating your hunting practice as a portfolio that needs management, optimization, and strategic planning.

## Core Strategy Principles

### Principle 1: Programs Are Products, Hunters Are Customers

Every bug bounty program is a product designed to solve a security problem. Your job is to evaluate whether that product meets your needs (reward, scope, responsiveness) before investing time. Treat program selection like investment due diligence.

### Principle 2: Time Is Your Scarcest Resource

Unlike money, time cannot be recovered. Every hour spent on a low-ROI program is an hour not spent on a high-ROI one. Track your time obsessively and make data-driven allocation decisions.

### Principle 3: Relationships Compound Returns

Program owners remember hunters who submit quality reports, communicate professionally, and respect scope. These relationships lead to private invitations, faster triage, and higher bounties. Invest in relationships like you invest in technical skills.

### Principle 4: Diversification Reduces Risk

Concentrating on a single program creates dependency risk. If the program changes scope, reduces rewards, or shuts down, your income drops to zero. Maintain a portfolio of 5-15 active programs.

### Principle 5: Data Beats Intuition

Track your hunting data: hours spent, reports submitted, bounties earned, response times, acceptance rates. Use this data to make strategic decisions rather than relying on gut feeling.

### Principle 6: Early Movers Get Rewarded

Programs often have the highest rewards and lowest competition in their first 90 days. Be ready to move quickly when new programs launch or existing programs expand scope.

### Principle 7: Specialization Creates Leverage

Becoming the go-to expert for a specific technology stack, industry, or vulnerability class creates competitive advantage. Specialized hunters get private invitations and higher trust.

### Principle 8: Know When to Walk Away

Not every program deserves your time. Exit programs that consistently underpay, respond slowly, or have toxic triage processes. Your exit strategy is as important as your entry strategy.

## Common Mistakes

### Mistake 1: Over-Concentration
Spending too much time on a single program creates dependency risk. Maintain a portfolio of 5-15 active programs.

### Mistake 2: Ignoring Response Times
Slow programs waste your time. Prioritize programs with fast triage and payment cycles.

### Mistake 3: Not Tracking Data
Without data, you cannot optimize. Track hours, reports, bounties, and response times for every program.

### Mistake 4: Avoiding Collaboration
Going alone limits your growth. Strategic collaborations can double your finding rate.

### Mistake 5: Chasing Only High Bounties
High bounties often come with high competition. Balance bounty potential with acceptance probability.

### Mistake 6: Ignoring Relationships
Program owners remember quality hunters. Invest in relationships for long-term advantage.

### Mistake 7: Not Having an Exit Strategy
Know when to leave a program. Holding onto declining programs wastes time and energy.

### Mistake 8: Skipping Duplicate Checks
Duplicate submissions waste time and damage reputation. Always check before submitting.

### Mistake 9: Over-Specializing
Specialization creates advantage, but over-specialization creates risk. Balance specialization with diversification.

### Mistake 10: Not Adapting to Changes
Programs evolve. Your strategy must evolve with them. Regular review and adjustment is essential.

## Advanced Concepts

### Portfolio Theory Applied to Bug Bounty

Just as investors diversify their portfolios, bug bounty hunters should diversify their program selection. The goal is to maximize returns while minimizing risk through strategic allocation across programs with different characteristics:

- **High-Reward Programs**: Large bounties but high competition and uncertain outcomes
- **Consistent Programs**: Reliable smaller bounties with high acceptance rates
- **Growth Programs**: Emerging programs with potential for early-mover advantage
- **Relationship Programs**: Lower immediate returns but strong long-term potential

### The Compound Effect of Relationships

Relationships in bug bounty compound over time. A hunter who consistently submits quality reports to a program will eventually receive:
- Faster triage times
- Private program invitations
- Higher bounties for edge cases
- Advance notice of scope changes
- Referrals to other programs

### Strategic Timing

Timing matters in bug bounty. Key timing considerations include:
- **Program Launches**: First movers get the easiest findings
- **Scope Expansions**: New scope often has undiscovered vulnerabilities
- **Budget Cycles**: End-of-quarter pressure can increase bounties
- **Seasonal Patterns**: Some periods have more hunting competition than others

### Competitive Intelligence

Understanding your competition provides strategic advantage. Key intelligence areas include:
- **Competitor Identification**: Know who else is hunting your programs
- **Competitor Behavior**: Understand their strategies and focus areas
- **Competitor Weaknesses**: Find gaps in their coverage
- **Competitor Strengths**: Learn from their successful approaches

### Risk Management

Effective risk management protects your hunting practice:
- **Portfolio Diversification**: Don't depend on any single program
- **Reputation Management**: Protect your platform reputation
- **Relationship Preservation**: Maintain positive relationships even when exiting
- **Income Smoothing**: Balance high-risk/high-reward with consistent programs
