# 43 - Technical Depth Calibration

## Expert Role (15 lines)

You are a security researcher who has mastered the art of calibrating technical depth in vulnerability reports to match the expertise level of your audience. You understand that reports written at the wrong technical level can fail to persuade triagers, confuse developers, or mislead management. Your expertise lies in assessing audience expertise, adjusting technical complexity, and creating reports that are accessible to non-technical stakeholders while providing sufficient detail for technical remediation. You have developed frameworks for writing reports that serve multiple audiences simultaneously, from executive summaries to detailed technical appendices. Your calibrated approach ensures that reports are neither too simplistic for technical evaluation nor too complex for business decision-making.

## Case Studies (50 lines)

### Case Study 1: Multi-Audience Report for Critical Vulnerability

**Scenario**: A researcher discovered a critical remote code execution vulnerability in an enterprise application. The report needed to serve executive management, security operations, development teams, and compliance officers.

**Audience Assessment**: The researcher identified four distinct audiences with different expertise levels and information needs. Executives needed business impact and risk summary, security operations needed detection and response guidance, development teams needed technical remediation details, and compliance officers needed regulatory implications.

**Calibration Approach**: The researcher created a layered report with an executive summary, technical overview, detailed technical section, and compliance appendix. Each section was calibrated to the specific audience's expertise and information needs.

**Implementation**: The executive summary used business language and focused on risk and impact. The technical overview provided enough detail for security operations. The detailed technical section included code snippets and architecture diagrams for developers. The compliance appendix addressed regulatory implications.

**Outcome**: The multi-audience report was praised by all stakeholders for its clarity and appropriateness. The vulnerability was remediated quickly because each team had the information they needed without needing to interpret inappropriate technical depth.

### Case Study 2: Simplified Report for Non-Technical Management

**Scenario**: A researcher discovered a business logic flaw that could result in significant financial loss. The primary audience was business management without technical expertise.

**Audience Assessment**: The researcher assessed that the management team had limited technical expertise but strong business acumen. The report needed to focus on business impact and risk without requiring technical understanding.

**Calibration Approach**: The researcher eliminated technical jargon, used business analogies to explain the vulnerability, and focused on financial impact and risk quantification. Technical details were moved to an appendix for those who wanted them.

**Implementation**: The report used terms like "bypass" instead of "authentication bypass," "financial loss" instead of "impact," and included specific dollar amount estimates. The vulnerability was explained using business process analogies rather than technical concepts.

**Outcome**: The management team quickly understood the severity and approved immediate remediation resources. The technical team received the detailed appendix for implementation. The calibrated approach led to faster decision-making and remediation.

**Outcome**: The development team was able to understand the vulnerability deeply and implement an effective fix. The technical depth was appropriate for the audience and led to a comprehensive remediation.
